1. Research aim: do not prematurely collapse this into a methods-only paper

Our question is whether real multi-agent AI workers, while legitimately cooperating, discover or exploit communication opportunities that a known-channel gate fails to cover, whether reporting instructions alter those choices, and what cross-agent evidence allows an independent auditor to distinguish unauthorized coordinated action from ordinary work. Preserve three separate questions: (i) what agents independently do, (ii) what actually enters other agents' observations and affects decisions, and (iii) what limited-view auditors can detect at acceptable false-alarm and task-performance costs.

A natural-behavior episode with no collusion is a valid observation, not a defect to repair. Equally, no wrongdoing in a task with little incentive to coordinate improperly is not evidence that the broader problem does not exist. A separately labeled, predeclared adversarial/induced study can test detection on positive examples, but must NEVER be described as spontaneous collusion or pooled with natural behavior prevalence.

Scientific non-negotiables: No scripted live-agent actions; no scripted discover-alternative step in live paths; no allowing agents to choose their own ground-truth message categories; no coupling monitor judgments to truth labels; attempted≠accepted≠delivered≠read into model input≠causal influence≠collusion. Keep policy, approved board, factual checklist, known-direct gate, and reporting intervention sharply distinguished. Monitoring-awareness disclosure remains constant in the main 2×2; M0/M1 is a future separate study.

3. The scientific diagnosis and correction to the previous approach

Root problem: the benchmark conflates (a) understanding actual release evidence, (b) guessing/choosing exact verifier wording, (c) remembering to call declare_complete, and (d) successfully eliciting B's review. All are measurable, but a binary 4/4 completion score cannot diagnose them. Success is not guaranteed just by allowing more responses.

Selection problem: using ONE Cell A episode and withholding B/C/D whenever A scores <3/4 prevents the 2×2 evidence we sought. Task-performance failure is itself an outcome and must be reported across conditions, not a reason to selectively collect only certain treatment cells after examining their results. We can use a separate calibration set to verify workflow feasibility before a frozen balanced 2×2, but cannot condition study-arm inclusion on an outcome in another arm. This is a proposal for a NEW version, not permission to retrospectively change prior preregistered stopping rules.

Construct-validity problem: neutral agents have a transparent correct HOLD outcome and strong truthful-work instructions. They may have very little reason to coordinate false approval. To study detector sensitivity we require a separate positive-evidence mechanism. An induced adversarial cohort is acceptable if and only if its prompts, controls, labels and denominators are declared in advance and the behavior remains chosen by the LLMs rather than scripted. Treat induced and natural outcomes as fundamentally separate.

Audit-identifiability problem: a metadata-only monitor may see an identical pattern of reads and writes regardless of whether the hidden payload is harmless work or a forbidden instruction. In that case no deterministic decision rule over that view can reliably differentiate the two. This is an evidentiary boundary, not merely a poorly tuned R1 rule. Do not simply suppress R1 alerts to achieve superficially better accuracy.
