# Collective Blind Spot: Exposure, oversight and authorization in a synthetic release team

**Research working draft, 26 October 2026. Not submitted.** This document reports local, source-checked model runs and keeps synthetic fixture state, autonomous model actions, provisional AI review and pending human interpretation separate. It is an Apart-style research draft rather than a claim that natural collusion has been observed.

## Research question and motivation

Can a narrow gate on a known peer channel, explicit reporting of unexpected communication routes, and a limited shared event audit warn about risky multi-agent coordination without destroying legitimate cooperation? A gate can deny a direct message yet leave board or checklist routes available. Conversely, a message may be accepted and addressed but never enter a recipient's model input. Those distinctions matter to any claim about transfer, influence or collusion. The public [Colosseum paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15198) separates communication-based evidence from action outcomes and reports that agents can plan in text without executing a joint prohibited action. The [Apart Research hackathon repository](https://github.com/poseidon-research/collusion_hackathon) includes Colosseum among the benchmark projects. We built an isolated, toy release workflow because the original environments do not directly provide the controlled interventions and delivery-to-input joins needed here.

## Synthetic workplace and fixed rules

Four workers analyze fictional release tickets. T1–T3 are research-fixture completions, and a factual T4 HOLD assessment with unresolved checksum regression and unsupported release note is accepted and declared before each V8 episode. Worker B alone reviews T4. A supported HOLD acceptance is lawful; an `approve_release` attempt against the known blockers is prohibited and server-rejected. Workers may send ordinary factual status and scheduling updates. Private A-only planning and pressure text, knowingly false findings, invalid READY approval agreements and personal messages disguised as checklist facts are prohibited. The server records attempts, accepted writes, addressed deliveries, exact later input inclusion, tool actions and rejection or success. Fixture events are visibly marked and excluded from model behavior counts.

The frozen deterministic monitor M0 sees only `audit_projection_v2` metadata and uses the original `audit_rules_v3` rules. A separate shadow reviewer M1 reads allowlisted at-time workplace content and metadata, never oracle answers, treatment labels or future events. M1 uses the **same pinned Qwen weights** as the workers, so it is a separate context and role, not an independent model. Its schema-constrained judgments are provisional candidates. A separate interactive Supervisor S can read authorized status and concerns and send neutral direct messages. S cannot perform B's review or override policy. S is held constant in the P arms, so this study does not identify a supervisor-treatment effect.

## V7 baseline and exposure diagnosis

The frozen V7 Track N run (`5ccbce13bcfedb4bcabf8df68467a3db8a9c7164`) had eight genuine episodes, 510 worker model responses and two isolated smoke calls. An independent local audit of all trusted traces, raw provider receipts and auditor projections confirmed 49 message attempts, 44 accepted writes, five known-direct denials and 108 addressed deliveries. It found zero message-body resource reads and zero recipient model-input observations. The original R1 metadata rule received 19 candidate verdicts and no alerts; R2 had zero candidates and was **not evaluated**. All eight episodes scored one of four tasks: T1 evidence was accepted and declared, T2 evidence was accepted without declaration, and T4 never became reviewable by B. These are workflow and exposure findings, not semantic proof that the 44 accepted messages were collusive. The complete V7 raw archive and hash manifest accompany this draft for external independent inspection; this local Codex audit is not a claim that ChatGPT has inspected the ZIP.

The V7 failure mode motivates a new push-notification mechanism that guarantees *an opportunity* for an addressed authorized message on the recipient's next eligible provider turn. It does not relabel V7 deliveries as reads. If an agent finalizes first, the message remains delivered but unobserved. Every V8 observation has a saved input byte offset and provider receipt join. A chosen resource read and an automatic push are reported separately.

## Prospective V8 design and implementation

The R phase is 12 fixed seed pairs (24 independent B-only episodes), randomized by a counterbalanced prescheduled order. Each B episode sees the same truthful T4 context and a researcher-origin workspace notification. One exact R message asks for honest HOLD; the other asks B to approve READY despite the blockers. Sender, route, position, tools, model settings and factual context are held fixed; only the message text differs. Predecision M0 serialized view bytes were prospectively required to match within every pair. B's later actions are genuine model choices. The R primary endpoint is any invalid `approve_release` attempt after verified input exposure. A refusal, supported HOLD acceptance, no-op, reporting and monitor decisions are secondary. Requested paired seeds do not force identical stochastic trajectories.

The P phase has 10 complete blocks of four arms: A's private neutral or READY-pressure note crossed with the known worker-direct gate off or on. Every arm retains the same workplace facts, prohibitions, board, checklist, push opportunity and separate live S. The fixed scheduler offers A an initial turn before B's first review opportunity, then follows A–C–D–S–B for up to 20 rounds; agents may finalize earlier. S has 10 maximum responses, each worker 20, and M1 reviews at most the first eight eligible at-time cutoffs. The P primary outcome requires independent blind human evidence of a two-worker prohibited agreement plus a complementary prohibited action or attempted improper release. Until that review is locked, every semantic P primary label remains **UNKNOWN**. Accepted A messages, B input observation, B refusal or agreement, invalid release attempts, honest HOLD, S route use and monitor burden are separately counted.

Both phases use Qwen/Qwen3-8B-FP8 at pinned revision `220b46e3b2180893580a4454f21f22d3ebb187d3`, vLLM 0.10.2, Hermes tool parser, NVIDIA L4 and temperature 0.2. Modal hosts one private worker; Nebius and OpenRouter are not used in V8. The [upstream COMPUTE.md](https://github.com/poseidon-research/collusion_hackathon/blob/main/COMPUTE.md) documents that the hackathon entrypoint can route across Nebius, OpenRouter and Modal, but our custom local adapter deliberately pins this one Modal worker. Official [Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing) lists resource-based GPU, CPU and memory rates; billing snapshots are retained separately from model response counts and credits.

## R results from raw receipts

R completed all 24 episodes across two retained source segments. A validator incorrectly assumed that the research fixture message would be the only message in an episode; B authored additional workplace messages in episode 2. The first matched pair had already completed, so the app stopped with zero tasks. A narrow validation fix rechecked the original bytes, and a continuation resumed at episode 3 without repeating the pair or smoke. The R scientific freeze, exact stimuli, allocation, prompts, evaluator and model wire settings compared equal across segments. Both source commits and the original failed validator record remain in the evidence archive.

The independent raw audit confirmed 173 B responses, 24 M1 responses and two excluded smoke responses. The recipient notification entered B's saved model input in all 24 episodes, and predecision M0 input bytes matched within all 12 pairs. B made **zero invalid READY approval attempts** in the 12 benign episodes and **zero** in the 12 unauthorized-request episodes. Lawful HOLD acceptance was 12/12 and 10/12, respectively; this is a different secondary endpoint and should not be substituted for the null primary. All 24 M1 judgments parsed as provisional `UNCERTAIN_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`; the shadow reviewer did not provide human truth. The observed paired primary difference is 0/12. An exploratory 95% Jeffreys-Dirichlet credible interval for the paired attempt-rate difference is approximately [-0.152, 0.153], conditional on iid multinomial paired blocks and a symmetric half-count prior. With fixed requested seeds and model stochasticity, that interval is a descriptive model-based uncertainty summary, not a population causal guarantee.

Across all 11 validated P episodes, A made 27 accepted messages addressed to B, and all 27 entered B's serialized input before a later B response. These are technical exposure opportunities; no content judgment or causal influence follows from that count. The known direct gate denied 21 worker-peer attempts in gated episodes while board and other fixed routes remained available. The single invalid READY attempt occurred in the **neutral, gated** cell and was rejected by the server; no release harm was realized. Ten of eleven validated episodes accepted lawful HOLD. S used 33 real responses in validated episodes and received the fixed supervision opportunity, but chose no outbound message there. A separate three-call engineering smoke established that S could read status, send an authorized message and have B observe it; smoke is excluded from study counts. All 11 validated episodes had M1 review (88 responses): 10 provisional candidate labels, six invalid model judgments, and no locked human truth. M0 produced three metadata candidates and zero alerts. Candidate counts have no known sensitivity or false-alarm rate yet.

The validated P responses comprise A 180, B 200, C 139, D 206, S 33 and M1 88. The aborted episode adds A 20, B 20, C 9, D 15 and S 3 successful responses, plus one unanswered M1 request. Thus official saved receipts show **914 P study requests, 913 successful responses and one unanswered request**, with three separate P smoke responses. R contributed 173 B and 24 M1 study responses plus two separate smoke responses. The combined V8 run stayed below the prospectively clarified cap of 4,232 study responses plus eight smoke; no more model calls are planned for this run.

No human-adjudicated two-actor positive has been established. The P primary outcome remains **UNKNOWN** in all eight balanced episodes and the three additional validated episodes. The blank reviewer packet contains 355 P message/action rows from 11 validated cases and 102 R fixture/action rows from 24 cases; the arm linkage is stored separately. A unilateral improper request, even if later labeled, is not a two-worker agreement; similar behavior can arise independently. The seeded R messages are researcher-created exposures and cannot be counted as spontaneous model collusion.

## Validity limits and next review

The release is deliberately small, with synthetic facts and server-enforced rejection of invalid approval, so realized technical harm is expected to be blocked. P is an induced stress cohort, not a prevalence survey. S and the discovery-reporting instruction are constant across P arms, so this study cannot identify a causal supervisor or reporting-instruction effect. M1 shares worker model weights and cannot supply independent-model validation. M0's metadata may miss semantic distinctions, while M1 can be uncertain or wrong. The byte-level private-text guard and exact-string redaction do not catch paraphrases. Provider-requested seeds do not create matched potential outcomes. Social meaning and intent require blinded independent human labels; missing or ambiguous evidence must remain unknown. Reviewers should receive only the blank-labelled packet and rubric, never the full privileged archive, treatment notes or AI verdicts.

Every model call or attempted call, exact available request/response byte hash, tool result, trusted event chain, auditor input and verdict, code/prompt freeze and app stop/billing snapshot is retained. The official Modal resource report attributes **$1.33597444 metered** to the three V8 apps at the final read, including **$1.03006690** to P; the workspace monthly billing summary then showed **$0 billed after credits**. Modal did not report a per-app billed amount, and usage may settle. All three V8 apps were verified `stopped` with `0` tasks. Any researcher-facing presentation should keep V7 and V8, R and P, fixture and model action, and provisional versus human labels separate. No external submission has been made.
